# Rolling-map pilot — notes

Ideas and observations written during the run. Nothing here changes the running pilot; anything worth doing is a future pilot.

## Before the run

- The pilot world evaluates probe A only. That is a deliberate narrowing (fitness needs only probe A) and the reason evaluations cost about 1.5 ms instead of 50 ms. A future experiment that wants the full signature (for a cache keyed on behaviour, or for convergence measures) pays the full price again.
- "Unknown accessibility counts as 0" was chosen over "known before unknown" because with S = 1 the latter would make the rule "prefer the mapped lineage" regardless of the measured value. With S = 1 the comparison that matters is between a lineage carrying a positive value and the rest; a mapped value of 0 does nothing. A future pilot could map S > 1 genomes at a lower K to compare values against each other.
- Inheritance never decays: a lineage carries its ancestor's measurement until it is re-measured. In the static arm this is the whole point (stale information). In rolling arms a refresh re-measures only the top class's lowest-slot genome, so other lineages keep older values. A decay rule (forget after D generations) is an obvious variant.
- The mapping events in the rolling arms are both measurement and intervention (the value is used), so the fresh-map prediction (analysis C) is confounded by use. A clean measurement would carry "measure-only" maps in the baseline arm, at a budget cost; a future pilot could afford it with the 30× cheaper evaluation.
- Rolling-8 is the most expensive arm and sets the allowance, so baseline runs 255 generations against rolling-8's 128. If baseline wins on final fitness, the honest reading is "extra ordinary search beat mapping at this K", not "mapping is useless": K = 512 is coarse.

## During the run

(appended as observed; counters only, no outcomes read before the run completes)

- 02:58 UTC (9 minutes in, 216 of 1,152 arm-runs final): the Node worker processes were growing by about 2 GB of RSS each, 29 GB in all, enough to exhaust the 62 GB before the end. Cause not investigated during the run (V8 letting the heap grow toward its default limit, or a retention in the probe path; the per-run cache is small). Workers were killed and relaunched at 03:00 UTC under `--max-old-space-size=1536` with a per-shard supervisor that relaunches a shard that exits before its closing line. The harness skips arm-runs that already carry a final line and restarts an interrupted one from zero, so with the same seeds and the frozen world the record is identical to an uninterrupted run. No design parameter changed. Logged in `results/relaunch.log` and at the `--- launch` markers in the shard logs.
- 03:37 UTC (1,013 of 1,152 arm-runs final, 25 of 32 shards finished): the six unfinished shards were serialised behind slow founders. The pre-run benchmark used pairs 0–31 (*P*, replicate 0), not the chosen twelve pairs, and some chosen founders' descendants run tens of thousands of instructions per probe, so a trace costs 100 ms instead of 1.5 ms (one shard was at 8.5 exec/s). Remaining arm-runs were relaunched one per worker, 32 at a time, with the same harness and parameters; an arm-run is independent of every other, deterministic under its seeds, and restarted from zero if it was in progress. The benchmark lesson for a real experiment: benchmark on the founders that will run, and shard at the arm-run level from the start.
- 03:40 UTC: the re-shard exposed a harness bug. The budget was derived from the arms a worker was given (allowance = mapping cost of the most mapping-hungry arm in `--arms`), so a worker running `baseline` alone got 8,192, `static` alone 8,704, `rolling16` alone 12,288. Every run file written with a budget other than 16,384 was deleted (all of them from the re-shard; the original shards always ran all six arms), the harness now takes `--budget` explicitly, and the remaining arm-runs were relaunched with `--budget 16384`. The equal-budget control holds for every file that remains: the analysis asserts it.
