A program's neutral descendants, which still do exactly what it does, do not have exactly its futures: every one of 1,319 of them, even eight bytes away, could reach in one mutation something its parent could not or had lost something it could, and the difference grows steadily with neutral distance.
The difference is small and specific: sixty bytes away, about one in thirteen of the exact mutations that matter has moved, the share of children that behave differently has not changed at all, and the whole shift is smaller than the gap between two unrelated programs that happen to do the same thing.
Two programs can do the same thing without being in the same place, and the place matters a little: a program's future is mostly fixed by what it does, and the rest, a fraction of a percent of it, by where its silent bytes have wandered.
