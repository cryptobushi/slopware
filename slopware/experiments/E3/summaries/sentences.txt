A program's neutral descendants, which still do exactly what it does under the frozen measurement, do not keep exactly its immediate futures: their one-mutant neighbourhoods diverge from its own, by a little at eight bytes and by more at every target after, in step with neutral distance.
The amount of adjacent variation does not change with the travel: the share of children that behave differently, and the share that outlive the genome, are the same sixty bytes away as at home, while the exact mutations that matter slowly rearrange, nine in ten still shared at sixty bytes and the rest moved.
Two programs can do the same measured thing without being in the same place: silent history does not add or remove possibility so much as rearrange which possibilities are one mutation away, changing the shape of the next step without changing the present.
