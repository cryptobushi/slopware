A program can be rewritten one byte at a time until none of its sixty-four bytes is the one it was born with, and still do exactly what it did.
Refuse every change that shortens a program's run and the run grows, from a median of three instructions to about twenty, and for some into loops that stop only when the gas is gone.
Half of those gains were impossible until silent changes had rearranged the program's background first: the neutrality E1 found is not dead space, it is the road.
