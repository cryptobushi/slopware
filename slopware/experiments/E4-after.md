# E4 — After

**Pre-registration. Signed 2026-10-11 (see Sign-off). Anchored on Ethereum before any proposal under the run seed (Amendment 1). No E4 population exists under the run seed; the only E4 proposals evaluated so far are the disclosed smoke tests (§11, Sign-off), which use another seed string and whose records are kept out of the E4 record.**

E4 follows E1 (`E1-neighbourhoods.md`), E2 (`E2-walks.md`) and E3 (`E3-somewhere-else.md`). E1 found that most one-byte changes leave a program's measured behaviour identical. E2 found that neutral space is traversable and that half of the lifespan improvements found under persistence selection were enabled by prior neutral substitutions. E3 found that neutral travel progressively rearranges which mutations are accessible next while the amount of adjacent variation stays approximately constant. E4 asks whether a quantity E3 already measured, frozen before any selection, predicts the response to selection: **does accessibility measured before selection predict what happens after selection begins?**

E4 is designed in `E4-design.md` (three rounds of outside review and the artist's decisions, all dated 2026-10-11). This document makes that design executable, auditable and unambiguous. It introduces nothing the design notes did not fix; the two places where it departs from a sentence of the notes are stated in the notes' dated addendum (§16 there). The predictor table `E4/predictors.json` was extracted from the sealed E3 record on 2026-10-11, before this document was written, and its hash is in §5.

## 1. Question

For 88 matched founder pairs, each a program *P* and a neutral descendant *N* with exactly the same measured behaviour but different bytes, E3 has already recorded the complete one-mutant neighbourhood of both. From that record a single number per founder is fixed: A1, the probability that one random single-byte proposal yields a finite child that runs longer. E4 starts identical population searches from *P* and from *N* under one disclosed selection rule and asks, across the 88 pairs, whether the difference in A1 predicts the difference in what the populations reach after 256 generations.

The mechanism under test is stated mechanically and nowhere else: neutral history → a different genotype → a different one-mutant neighbourhood → possibly a different response to the same selection rule. Execution is deterministic; nothing remembers anything. The claim E4 can make, if the record supports it, is that an accessibility difference produced by these neutral histories predicted the later response under selection. It is not that neutral history caused adaptation; *P* and *N* were not assigned their histories at random.

The important null is not "nothing evolves". It is: **neutral history, as measured by E3, does not predict subsequent selection outcomes.**

## 2. Design in one paragraph

From each of 176 founders (88 *P*, 88 *N*), 16 independent replicate populations of 64 individuals run for 256 generations under one rule: each generation, 64 proposals are made by choosing a parent slot uniformly and changing exactly one byte; proposals are evaluated from the same clean world state as E1–E3; the 64 fittest finite individuals among survivors and offspring continue, ties resolved by a seeded order; a program that runs out of gas after more than 32 instructions never continues. Fitness is the probe-A instruction count of a finite run. Generation 1 of every population is a sample from a neighbourhood E3 has already measured in full, and is checked against it. After the run, the per-pair difference in mean final log₂ best fitness is correlated with the per-pair difference in A1 across the 88 pairs. That correlation is the result.

## 3. Hypotheses and pre-specified measurements

Definitions are in §5; outcomes are listed in advance; none is "success". Every resampling procedure is seeded as §7.0 says.

**Primary — prediction.** Spearman ρ across the 88 pairs between ΔA1 = A1(*N*) − A1(*P*) and ΔB = *b*₂₅₆(*N*) − *b*₂₅₆(*P*), where *b*₂₅₆ is the mean over the 16 replicates of log₂ best finite fitness at generation 256. Ties take average ranks. If ΔB has zero variance across the pairs, ρ is undefined and is reported as undefined, not as zero. Uncertainty: 95% percentile bootstrap interval (numpy "linear" percentiles at 2.5 and 97.5) from 10,000 resamples of the 88 pairs with replacement, using the fixed index sets of §7.0; a resample with zero variance in either ranked variable has undefined ρ, is excluded from the interval and counted. Direction stated in advance: positive. **ρ = 0.3 is pre-specified as the smallest effect considered scientifically substantial.** It is an interpretive reference and not a success threshold: the result is the estimate with its interval, read against that reference. For the outcome labels below only: *primary positive* means the interval excludes zero with a positive estimate; *primary null* means the interval covers zero; an interval excluding zero with a negative estimate is outcome F.

**Secondary — founder identity.** Do matched founders produce more different outcomes than replicate stochasticity alone? Per pair, *s*_pooled = √((*s*²_P + *s*²_N)/2) with sample variances (*n* − 1) of *B*₂₅₆ over the 16 replicates of each founder. *S* = (mean over pairs of |ΔB|) ÷ (mean over pairs of *s*_pooled). Null: within each pair the 32 populations are relabelled *P*/*N* at random (16 each), 10,000 times with the seeded generator of §7.0; *S** is recomputed each time; one-sided p = (#{*S** ≥ *S*_obs} + 1)/(10,001). If every pair's replicates agree exactly the denominator is zero, *S* is undefined, and that absence of replicate variation is the reported result. Also reported: the number of pairs whose 16 *N*-populations all lie strictly above or all strictly below the 16 *P*-populations; the tie-free closed form 88 × 2/C(32,16) is an upper bound on the null expectation, and the null distribution of the count comes from the same permutation (P_perm(count ≥ observed) reported). A signed mean ΔB is not used: neutral history has no global direction. *Secondary positive* means p < 0.05; this test says whether founder identity matters, not whether E3 knew which way.

**Tertiary — the prediction horizon.** ρ_g, the primary statistic recomputed at every generation *g* from 1 to 256 with the same 10,000 index sets, drawn as one trajectory with its band; for every *g* the number of pairs with ΔB_g = 0 is reported and an undefined ρ_g is drawn as a gap. Interpreted descriptively; no per-generation test and no derived "half-life". The prediction is expected to be most directly expressed early, because generation-1 proposals are sampled from the neighbourhood E3 measured; whether a finite population of 64 draws realises that expectation is itself empirical.

**Also reported, pre-specified.** (a) Secondary predictors A2 (best accessible improvement) and A3 (neutral probability), each with its own ρ against ΔB and the same 10,000-resample interval; A4 (loop accessibility) was defined prospectively and is identically zero across all 176 founders in the frozen table, admits no correlation test, and that is reported. (b) Loop-class incidence per founder, per generation, descriptively. (c) Convergence: for each population the empirical frequencies *p* over signature hashes of its 64 final slots; total variation distance TV(*p*, *q*) = ½ Σ|*p* − *q*|; per pair, the mean over the 16 × 16 *P*–*N* population pairs (between) and the mean over the 2 × C(16,2) = 240 replicate pairs within founders (within); the two means per pair and overall, with the difference's pair-bootstrap interval; descriptive. (d) Strata: ρ with its interval and *S* with its permutation p, by parent lifespan (3; ≥ 4) and by set (genesis; control), descriptively, no test. (e) Leave-one-pair-out on the primary ρ. (f) Logical proposals, physical executions and cache hits, per worker and in total. (g) E3's per-pair neighbourhood distance *D*_sig (`E4/pair-distances.json`, computed from E3's published signature histograms) beside ΔB, descriptively.

**Outcomes, written now.** A founder *adapts* if any of its 16 replicates has *B*₂₅₆ > *B*₀.
- **A.** No founder adapts: ΔB has zero variance, ρ is undefined and reported as undefined. Unexpected given E2; the mechanics are examined and reported, not tuned.
- **B.** Primary null and secondary not positive. *E3 showed that neutral history changes immediate accessibility; E4 found that those differences do not measurably predict adaptation under this selection regime.* Reported with the observed interval; the synthetic simulations of the design notes expect intervals about 0.4 wide at 88 pairs, and the actual interval, not the simulation, says what magnitudes remain compatible with the record.
- **C.** Primary positive, estimate and interval reported against ρ = 0.3, with the horizon showing how long E3's information lasts.
- **D.** Secondary positive, primary null. Matched founder identity matters, but E3's one-mutant accessibility predictor does not explain which founder does better; the relevant difference is therefore not captured by A1. No causal claim about history is made: *P* and *N* were not randomly assigned their histories (§1).
- **E.** Primary null, with a descriptively positive early prediction horizon that later disappears: for one or more *g* < 256, ρ_*g* is positive and its pointwise bootstrap band excludes zero, while the generation-256 primary interval covers zero. The horizon is descriptive and its 256 pointwise bands are not multiplicity-adjusted; this outcome is therefore reported as an early-prediction/convergence pattern, not as a second confirmatory test.
- **F.** Anything else, including a negative primary interval, reported descriptively against this list, primary test unchanged.

## 5. Definitions, frozen

1. **Fitness (F2, the longest finite run).** An evaluation is **finite** if the program is instantiable and probe A does not end out of gas after more than 32 traced instructions; a run that ends out of gas at 32 or fewer instructions is finite. A program whose probe A ends out of gas after more than 32 traced instructions is in the **loop class** and has no fitness. The fitness of a finite program is its probe-A instruction count; *k* ranks above *j* iff *k* > *j*. A loop-class program never ranks above any finite program and never continues. The loop class is E2's frozen operational classification, kept for continuity; the name does not assert cycle detection. Disclosed: *we select for the longest finite run.* Reported as log₂(count).
2. **Signature.** E1 §6, unchanged.
3. **Proposal.** From a parent genome *g*: position *i* uniform in 0..63, value *v* uniform over the 255 bytes other than *g*[*i*]; 16,320 equally probable proposals. A proposal whose child begins with `0xEF` is **uninstantiable** (E3's tables call it `refused`): recorded, not evaluated, not a candidate, not replaced. Genome length is 64 bytes (constitution amendment 2, `ROADMAP.md` Part X-D; `E4/world-v0.json` records `genomeLength: 64`).
4. **Population.** A multiset of 64 **individual slots**, indexed 0..63. Generation 0: 64 slots carrying the founder. Duplicate genomes may occupy several slots and each slot has its own reproductive weight.
5. **Generation *n* (1..256).** 64 proposals, proposal *s* (0..63) from the parent in survivor slot `parentIndex(n, s)`; evaluation of each instantiable child; then selection, which produces the slots of generation *n*.
6. **Selection.** Candidates are the 64 survivor slots of generation *n* − 1 and every finite offspring of generation *n*. Each candidate receives a **tie key** (§5.7). Candidates sort by fitness descending, then tie key ascending, the key compared as a 256-bit big-endian unsigned integer (bytewise lexicographic); on an equal key, which is checked and expected never to occur, a survivor precedes an offspring and a lower slot precedes a higher one. The first 64 become slots 0..63 of generation *n* in that order. Nothing is deduplicated before ranking. Founders are finite and survivors remain candidates, so at least 64 finite candidates always exist and the population is exactly 64 every generation; *B_n* ≥ *B_{n−1}* follows and is asserted per generation (violation halts). In one sentence: neutral mutations can enter through the random resolution of fitness ties; fitness-decreasing mutations cannot displace a fitter survivor; fitness-increasing mutations are preferentially retained.
7. **Seeds, domain-separated.** The run seed string is `E4-2026-10-11`. For pair index *q* (0..87), founder *f* (0 = *P*, 1 = *N*), replicate *r* (0..15), generation *n* (1..256) and slot *s* (0..63), the stream value for a domain is `keccak256(utf8("E4-2026-10-11-" ‖ domain) ‖ uint32(q) ‖ uint8(f) ‖ uint32(r) ‖ uint32(n) ‖ uint32(s))`, all fields big-endian. Domains: `parent`: bytes 0..7 as big-endian uint64 mod 64 → `parentIndex(n, s)`; `position`: bytes 0..7 mod 64 → *i*; `value`: bytes 0..7 mod 255 → *w*, then *v* = *w* if *w* < *g*[*i*] else *w* + 1; `tie`: the full 32 bytes, the key of offspring *s* in the selection that produces generation *n*; `tie-survivor`: the full 32 bytes with *s* the survivor slot, the key of that survivor in the same selection, drawn afresh every generation. The uint64 reductions have bias below 2⁻⁵⁶, accepted and stated. The seeds of the analysis-side generators are in §7.0.
8. **Cache.** A genotype has one phenotype under world v0 because every evaluation starts from the identical post-genesis state at the same address. Each worker process keeps a cache keyed on the 64 genome bytes; a genotype executed in two workers is executed twice, and physical executions are counted per worker and summed. The cache of a worker is empty at start; each founder is physically executed and cached before its generation 1; every other genome is physically executed on its first encounter in that worker, in generation 1 or later, and looked up thereafter, never re-executed and never counted as a second measurement. Harness failures are never cached. The world id, address and harness version are constants of the run written into every population's header. The cache does not collapse slots or change reproductive weight. Logical proposals and physical executions are both counted.
9. **Outcome statistics.** *B_g*(population) = log₂ of the best finite fitness among its 64 slots at generation *g*, with *B*₀ = log₂ of the founder's count. *b_g*(founder) = mean of *B_g* over its 16 replicates. ΔB_g(pair) = *b_g*(*N*) − *b_g*(*P*). ΔB = ΔB₂₅₆.
10. **Predictors, from the sealed E3 record.** `E4/predictors.json`, sha256 `810759357e401957e5d23738c6411eb90704aa4bf63445c8f3cf8d4c6251e907`, extracted 2026-10-11 by `scripts/e4_predictors.py` from a read-only copy of snapshot `slopware-e3-record-20261011` (ROOT `70471484778c5c91a8e7a299524af13d127bf491d70a0a62e7e53cd2628b8205`), before this document. Per founder, over its 16,320 proposals as E3 recorded them (16,319 placeable children and one `refused`): **A1** = *u*/16,320, *u* the placeable children that are finite and whose probe-A count exceeds the founder's; **A2** = log₂ of the largest finite child count; **A3** = children with the founder's exact signature / 16,320; **A4** = loop-class children / 16,320; **F_f**, the complete proposal distribution (finite counts by probe-A count, loop, refused, harness failure); and the sha256 of the canonical child table (lines `pos,val,cls,sigH,life,loop` sorted by position then value, the serialisation of `scripts/e4_predictors.py`, reproduced by `scripts/e4_tables.py`). Properties, stated before the run: A1 over all 176 founders from 0.00055 to 0.01471, median 0.00662; ΔA1 from −65 to +61 children, median +1, 50 distinct values, 3 exact zeros, 46 positive, 39 negative; A4 = 0 for every founder; A2 from 2.32 to 3.70. The table changes nothing about which pairs run or how. E3's per-pair *D*_sig is in `E4/pair-distances.json` (`scripts/e4_distances.py`, from the E3 summaries), descriptive only.

## 6. Environment and the fixed harness

World v0, frozen as in E1–E3 (`E4/world-v0.json`, a copy of `E3/world-v0.json` with the length parameter added), every genotype evaluated from the same post-genesis snapshot at the same address. `harnessVersion e4-1` is `e3-1`'s placement, probe and signature code path unchanged, driven by the E4 population loop; the only differences between `src/e3.ts` and `src/e4.ts` are the loop and the record, and `src/e4.ts` is hashed in the Sign-off. **Instrument errors are not phenotypes:** a probe or receipt timeout, RPC error or instrumentation exception is retried up to three times after the first attempt, each from a fresh revert; a proposal that still fails is a **harness failure**, recorded, excluded from candidates, counted, listed and never cached; the slot is not refilled. A **shard** is one worker process and the populations it runs. A cumulative harness-failure rate above 0.1% of a shard's physical executions, checked after every generation once the shard has made 1,000 physical executions, halts that shard; the other shards continue.

**Generation-1 deterministic check (halts on failure).** Before the run, each founder's canonical child table is produced from a read-only copy of the E3 snapshot by `scripts/e4_tables.py`, and its sha256 must equal the hash in `predictors.json`; `src/e4.ts` re-hashes each table it loads. Before a founder's generation 1, its own E4 evaluation (full signature and probe-A count) is compared with E3's summary record for that genome; mismatch halts. For every generation-1 proposal that is evaluated (physically or from the cache), the E4 evaluation is compared with E3's row for that (founder, position, value): `cls`, `sigH` (the signature hash), `life` (the probe-A count) and `loop`. A generation-1 harness failure, or an uninstantiable proposal whose E3 class is not `refused`, is a mismatch (E3 recorded no harness failures). Any disagreement halts the shard until the cause is written down. This is instrument validity and is not statistical.

**Halt semantics.** After any halt, the cause is written in a dated amendment below the Sign-off; the whole run then restarts from generation 0 under the same seeds; partial records are discarded and listed in that amendment. Nothing seen in a partial record changes anything in this document.

**Reproducibility subset.** Replicate 0 of every founder (176 populations) is re-run from its seeds on a second machine after the run and before analysis. Compared, for every generation: parent slot, position, value, child hash, class, fitness, the 64 survivor slots in order, *B_g*; excluded from the comparison: cache-hit flags and execution counts, which depend on the worker's history. Expected: identical. A harness failure in either run at any (population, generation, slot), or any other difference, is reported as a difference and investigated before analysis, with the finding in a dated amendment; it does not by itself void the record.

## 7. Analysis, pre-specified

0. **Seeds on the analysis side.** Every resampling generator in `scripts/e4_analysis.py` is numpy `default_rng` seeded with bytes 0..7 of keccak256 of a domain string as a big-endian uint64: `E4-2026-10-11-bootstrap` (the 10,000 pair-resample index sets, drawn once and reused for the primary, every ρ_g, A2, A3, convergence and strata), `E4-2026-10-11-permutation` (the 10,000 within-pair relabellings, reused for *S* and the strata), `E4-2026-10-11-calibration` (§7.5).
1. **Record, per population:** a header (pair, founder, replicate, founder bytes, founder count and signature hash, harness constants, seed string); per generation the 64 proposals (parent slot, position, value, child signature hash, probe-A count, loop flag, cache hit, harness failure, uninstantiable, generation-1 check flag), the selection as candidate references, the 64 survivor fitnesses in slot order, *B_g*, loop and failure counts; a final line with the 64 survivors' bytes, signature hashes and fitnesses and the worker's counts. Per worker, the cache table (genome bytes → signature hash, probe-A count, loop flag, reach flags). Every genome of every generation is reconstructible from the founder and the proposals; child hashes are the signature hash and the genome bytes are 64 bytes.
2. **Primary:** as §3, with the fixed index sets; leave-one-pair-out.
3. **Secondary:** *S*, its permutation p, the separated-pair count and its permutation p.
4. **Tertiary:** ρ_g for *g* = 1..256 with the band, the zero-ΔB_g counts, undefined values as gaps.
5. **Generation-1 calibration (reported, not a halt).** Generation 1 consists of 64 i.i.d. proposals from a frozen founder-specific distribution. Given the anchored seed string and the sealed tables, every population's generation 1 is computable before the run, so after the deterministic check has passed these statistics can only detect a seed-stream or bookkeeping defect, and a tail event is read as that, never as a harness or biological signal. *K*, the number of populations whose generation 1 contains at least one finite offspring whose probe-A count exceeds the founder's, has *p_i* = 1 − (1 − *u_i*/16,320)^64 per population and is evaluated against its **exact Poisson-binomial** reference distribution over the 2,816 populations; investigate if P(*K* ≤ *K*_obs) ≤ 10⁻⁴ or P(*K* ≥ *K*_obs) ≤ 10⁻⁴. *M* is the mean over populations of *m_i* = *F*(*x_i*⁻) + ½[*F*(*x_i*) − *F*(*x_i*⁻)], where *x_i* is the observed best generation-1 finite offspring count (or the category "none") and *F*(*k*) = *F_f*(*k*)^64 is the exact best-of-64 distribution with *F_f* the founder's proposal CDF over the 16,320 proposals, non-finite outcomes ranked below every finite count and "none" the lowest category. Its **Monte Carlo** reference is 10,000 simulated runs in which each population's best-of-64 value is drawn by inverse CDF from *F* with one uniform from the `calibration` generator (distributionally identical to 64 proposal draws); the 10,000 simulated *M* values are written to the record; investigate if *M*_obs lies below the minimum or above the maximum of them.
6. **Convergence, loop incidence, A2, A3, strata, *D*_sig, compute counts:** as §3.
7. **Script:** `scripts/e4_analysis.py`, frozen at the signing commit; no analysis is added after the run without being labelled exploratory.

## 8. Compute, record, stopping rule

176 founders × 16 replicates × 256 generations × 64 proposals = 46,137,344 logical proposals, plus 176 founder evaluations; physical executions fewer by the cache. E3 placed 22,960,833 children in four hours on one c-32 droplet (about $9 all in); E4 is planned on the same class for about eight hours, about $20, with a read-only copy of the E3 snapshot mounted for the tables and destroyed afterward. Record: as §7.1, roughly 5 GB; manifest, root hash and anchoring as E1 §8; snapshot and teardown as E2 and E3, the droplet destroyed only after the record is sealed, analysed and snapshotted, and the E4 record volume kept. **Stopping rule (constitution decision 7):** every population runs exactly 256 generations and stops nowhere else; nothing is extended, restarted, pruned or re-seeded after any trajectory is seen, except the restart-from-zero of §6 after a halt. Never a stop: stagnation, convergence, loops, no adaptation, strong adaptation, any look of any result. Extinction cannot occur.

## 9. Confounders named in advance

- **Harness drift between E3 and E4**, which would void the prediction: the generation-1 deterministic check.
- **Heavy-tailed fitness**: the loop class never continues; statistics on log₂ and ranks.
- **Ties as the drift mechanism**: fully specified seeded tie keys over individual slots.
- **Pseudo-replication**: the pair is the unit; replicates estimate within-founder spread only.
- **Founder heterogeneity across pairs**: removed by the within-pair contrast; reported as a stratum.
- **Small expected effects**: at sixty bytes *P* and *N* still share 92.5% of their exact non-neutral mutations; the design states its precision and reports the observed interval.
- **Convergence by strong selection**: outcome E, a result.
- **Analyst freedom**: one primary predictor, one outcome, one test, named here; seeded resampling; binary outcome labels fixed in §3; no composite scores; A4 not replaced.

## 11. What must not happen

- No founder is chosen, kept or dropped by anything but the E3 rule. No proposal is evaluated under the run seed before signing and anchoring. **Smoke tests** are the one carve-out: seed string `E4-smoke`, pairs 0 and 1, replicate 0, three generations, on the laptop and once more on the droplet with the tables before the run; their records are kept out of the E4 record and nothing in this document changes because of them except implementation defects they expose, which are listed in the Sign-off.
- Nothing is rewarded but the longest finite run. No CREATE, CALL, self-inspection, novelty or "life" enters fitness.
- No crossover, no AI-guided mutation, no changing environment, no second objective, no length change.
- No walk-1 or walk-2 endpoint is used.
- Nothing is placed on a public chain; the installer is not touched.
- No trajectory beyond generation 1 is looked at before all 2,816 populations have finished; the halting checks of §6 read generation-1 fields and instrument counters only.

### Amendment 2 — 2026-10-11, halt of the first run: the instrument's response cap; restart from zero

**Halt.** The first run under the run seed started 2026-10-12 01:18 UTC (2026-10-11 21:18 in the artist's time zone) on the c-32 droplet `slopware-e4`, 32 shards, commit 9bd9403, and was halted by the operator on the artist's word at 01:51 UTC, before any pre-registered halt condition fired: 94 populations opened, 59 final, 20,944 generation lines, about 1.27 million executions, 13 harness failures on three shards (0.001% overall; the highest shard 9 in about 15,000 executions, 0.06%, against the 0.1% rule). **Cause.** All 13 harness failures carried the same error: `HTTP response body exceeded the size limit. Max: 10485760 bytes. Received: 23–28 MB (viem 2.57.3)`. The RPC transport in `src/chain.ts`, shared by E1–E4 and unchanged since E1, used viem's default 10 MiB cap on response bodies. The probe's `debug_traceCall` step trace costs about 87 bytes per executed instruction, so a probe-A run past about 120,000 instructions, finite or not, cannot be returned, is retried three times with the same result, and is recorded as a harness failure and excluded from candidates. That is systematic, not random: it removes exactly the children F2 rewards (long finite runs) and misclassifies long loops that burn the whole 1,000,000 gas (about 245,000 instructions, 21 MB) as instrument errors instead of loop class. E1–E3 never met the cap: E3's longest finite child ran 13 instructions and no E3 child produced a trace over 10 MiB (E3 recorded no harness failures), so the sealed predictor table and the generation-1 tables are unaffected.

**Fix.** `src/chain.ts`: `maxResponseBodySize` set to 256 MiB on both transports; nothing else in the harness changes, and no value measured under the old cap can change, since a trace that fit under 10 MiB is returned identically. The largest trace a 1,000,000-gas probe can produce is about 30 MB. `harnessVersion` becomes `e4-2`, written in every header; the sentence of §6 naming `e4-1` is superseded by this amendment. Verification: `scripts/e4_tracecap.ts`, through the real transport and tracer, measured a tight loop at 244,752 executed instructions, outcome out of gas (loop class), in 0.6 s, and a two-instruction program as before; a third laptop smoke (seed `E4-smoke`, pairs 0 and 1, replicate 0, three generations) was identical to the first two in every record line and in its 772-genome cache.
