Two programs that do exactly the same measured thing, sixty bytes apart, do not get equally far under the same selection rule: which founder a population started from changes where it ends, by about half a within-founder standard deviation, more than relabelling the replicates can produce.
E3's map of what is one mutation away predicts the first few dozen generations and not the end: the correlation between predicted and observed differences climbs to 0.43 around generation 30 and is not distinguishable from zero by generation 256, where the interval, −0.03 to 0.39, neither confirms nor excludes the 0.3 the design called substantial.
Every one of the 2,816 populations found a longer finite run than its founder, most by two to three doublings and one by fifteen, and the final populations are almost disjoint in signature even between replicates of the same founder: founder identity sets how far a population gets, not which program it arrives at.
