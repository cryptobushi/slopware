E4 · after

the fourth experiment, pre-registered, run once

The answer first. Across the 88 pairs, the rank correlation between E3's predicted difference in accessibility and the observed difference in mean final log₂ best fitness is 0.18, 95% interval −0.03 to +0.39. The interval covers zero, so by the rule written in advance the prediction is read as null at generation 256; it also covers the 0.3 the design named as the smallest substantial effect, so the record neither confirms nor excludes an effect of that size. Founder identity itself matters: matched founders with identical behaviour reach different mean final fitness, S = 0.47, permutation p = 1/10,001. And the prediction is not absent everywhere: recomputed generation by generation, the correlation climbs to 0.43 around generation 31 with its band clear of zero from generation 2 to 63, then falls back. Outcome D.

E3 found that a program's neutral descendants, which still do exactly what it does under the frozen measurement, no longer have exactly its immediate futures: the one-byte changes that would alter them are not the same changes. E4 asks what follows. From each of 88 matched pairs of founders, a program and a neutral descendant of it sixty bytes away with the identical measured signature, the lab ran 16 populations of 64 for 256 generations under one rule: each generation 64 proposals, one byte changed each; the 64 longest finite runs continue; ties are broken by a seeded order; a program that runs out of gas after more than 32 instructions never continues; nothing else is rewarded. Three temporal regions: generation 0 is known, since every founder's behaviour was measured in E3; generation 1's proposal space was mapped in advance, since E3 had already measured every one-byte change of every founder, and every one of the run's 180,224 generation-1 proposals was checked against that map; generation 2 onward is unmapped.

The question, the pairs, the selection rule, the seeds, the analysis, the outcome labels and the picture's rules were written down and anchored on Ethereum before the first proposal. Nothing below was chosen after seeing the data; where the frozen analysis script turned out to be defective, the defect is reported, not repaired.

what was asked, and what the record says

One primary measurement, one secondary, one tertiary, stated before the run with their outcome labels listed in advance; none is called success. Each is read by its own rule and nothing else.

The instrument: 0 harness failures in 46,104,296 executions; all 180,224 generation-1 proposals matched E3's sealed tables; 0 tie-key collisions. The pre-registered reproducibility subset, replicate 0 of every founder re-run on a second machine before analysis, found 153 of 176 populations identical in every field of every generation and 23 not. The cause is in the harness: world v0 never froze anvil's block context, so a program that reads TIMESTAMP, PREVRANDAO or BLOCKHASH can evaluate differently on another machine, about eight proposals in a million do, and in 13 of the 176 populations that reached the selection and changed the final population. Reported in Amendment 6 of the protocol document, with the 23 genomes and their re-evaluations; by the pre-registered rule it does not void the record, and nothing in the analysis changes because of it.

primary · prediction

Spearman ρ across the 88 pairs between ΔA1, E3's difference in upward accessibility (the fraction of one-byte changes that outlive the founder), and ΔB, the difference in mean final log₂ best finite fitness over 16 replicates, N minus P in both. 95% percentile bootstrap interval from 10,000 pre-seeded resamples of the pairs. Direction stated in advance: positive. ρ = 0.3 pre-specified as the smallest substantial effect, an interpretive reference and not a threshold. Labels: primary positive if the interval excludes zero with a positive estimate; primary null if it covers zero.

ρ = 0.183, interval −0.026 to +0.385; 0 pairs with ΔB = 0; 0 undefined resamples. Leave-one-pair-out: 0.162 to 0.221. ΔB over the 88 pairs: 39 positive, 49 negative, mean 0.00, sd 0.67 doublings.

primary null: 0.18 (−0.03 to +0.39), the interval covers zero and covers 0.3 The interval is 0.41 wide, as the design notes expected at 88 pairs; what magnitudes remain compatible with the record is what it says.

secondary · founder identity

Do matched founders produce more different outcomes than replicate stochasticity alone? S = mean over pairs of |ΔB| divided by the mean pooled within-founder standard deviation of B₂₅₆. Null: the 32 populations of each pair relabelled P/N at random, 10,000 times, pre-seeded; one-sided p. Also the number of pairs whose 16 N populations all lie strictly above or all strictly below the 16 P populations. Secondary positive if p < 0.05. This says whether founder identity matters, not whether E3 knew which way.

S = 0.472; permutation p = 1/10,001 (10,000 relabellings, none reaching the observed S). Fully separated pairs: 0 (tie-free bound on the null expectation 2.9e-07; permutation p 1.00).

secondary positive: S = 0.47, p = 1/10,001 No causal claim about history: P and N were not randomly assigned their histories.

outcome

Outcome D, by the list written before the run: primary null, secondary positive. Matched founder identity matters, but E3's one-mutant accessibility predictor does not explain which founder does better; the relevant difference is therefore not captured by A1. The early-prediction pattern of outcome E is present as well and is reported descriptively above.

in three sentences

Two programs that do exactly the same measured thing, sixty bytes apart, do not get equally far under the same selection rule: which founder a population started from changes where it ends, by about half a within-founder standard deviation, more than relabelling the replicates can produce.

E3's map of what is one mutation away predicts the first few dozen generations and not the end: the correlation between predicted and observed differences climbs to 0.43 around generation 30 and is not distinguishable from zero by generation 256, where the interval, −0.03 to 0.39, neither confirms nor excludes the 0.3 the design called substantial.

Every one of the 2,816 populations found a longer finite run than its founder, most by two to three doublings and one by fifteen, and the final populations are almost disjoint in signature even between replicates of the same founder: founder identity sets how far a population gets, not which program it arrives at.
