SLOPWARE installs random bytes on Ethereum as executable software. The bytes are not interpreted, corrected, or selected for what they can do. Each becomes a program at its own address.


The art is whatever happens next.

Before this installer existed we asked a plain question: what does random bytecode do? One million programs of sixty-four random bytes were installed on a private copy of Ethereum and called, with every instruction traced. Nothing was filtered, weighted or retried.



—programs of sixty-four random bytes
—refused by Ethereum (first byte 0xEF)
—installed
—halted on their first instruction
—instructions, the longest any program ran
—answered differently to what they were sent
—wrote something to storage that survived
—returned data
—ran out of gas, by appetite, never by looping
—called another contract



Nearly every random program dies at once, for one of two reasons: the byte is not an instruction, or the instruction reaches for something that is not on the stack. About one in five survives its first instruction, and one in five of those survives the next. The odds of a long life shrink by that factor at every step, whatever the length of the program.


The rare ones are the point. A handful answered differently when sent money or data. Five remembered something. Seventeen tried to create another contract, and none succeeded. Roughly one program in ten thousand does anything a person would call behavior, and nobody can tell which from the bytes.


Every program on this page is drawn from that same distribution. Most of it will not run. Some of it will. Nobody knows which.


Method: random bytes placed by CREATE behind the same eleven-byte loader the installer uses, code verified byte for byte, then called five ways under a one-million-gas ceiling. The harness and its records are in the repository.
