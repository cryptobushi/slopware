E3 · somewhere else

the third experiment, pre-registered, run once

E2 found that a program can be rewritten byte by byte, executed code included, without changing anything it does, and that half of every improvement found under selection had been enabled by such silent changes beforehand. E3 asks the question underneath: when a program's neutral descendants still do exactly what it does, do they still have the same futures? From each of the 88 E2 parents, 42 collected and 46 fresh random, the lab took three fresh neutral walks and froze each at the first accepted step where it had moved 8, 16, 32, 48 and 60 bytes away from its parent. Then it did what E1 did, exhaustively: every one-byte change of every parent and every endpoint, 16,319 children each, placed on a clean copy of Ethereum and called five ways with every instruction traced. The walks make the specimens. The neighbourhoods are the measurement.

Nothing is selected and nothing is rewarded. The five measurements, their decision rules, the references, the reproducibility checks, the analysis and the picture's rules were written down and anchored on Ethereum before the first attempt. Nothing below was chosen after seeing the data.
