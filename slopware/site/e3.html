E3 · somewhere else

the third experiment, pre-registered, run once

E2 found that a program can be rewritten byte by byte, executed code included, without changing anything it does, and that half of every improvement found under selection had been enabled by such silent changes beforehand. E3 asks the question underneath: when a program's neutral descendants still do exactly what it does, do they still have the same futures? From each of the 88 E2 parents, 42 collected and 46 fresh random, the lab took three fresh neutral walks and froze each at the first accepted step where it had moved 8, 16, 32, 48 and 60 bytes away from its parent. Then it did what E1 did, exhaustively: every one-byte change of every parent and every endpoint, 16,319 children each, placed on a clean copy of Ethereum and called five ways with every instruction traced. The walks make the specimens. The neighbourhoods are the measurement.

Nothing is selected and nothing is rewarded. The five measurements, their decision rules, the references, the reproducibility checks, the analysis and the picture's rules were written down and anchored on Ethereum before the first attempt. Nothing below was chosen after seeing the data.

SOMEWHERE ELSE. One row per parent, 88 rows in parents.json order. Left to right: the parent, then its first walk's neutral descendants at 8, 16, 32, 48 and 60 bytes' distance, each drawn as a walk from its bytes with THREADS' pen: faint, the whole program; black, the part it executes; red, three or more instructions. Beneath each, a bar of 64 cells, one per byte position, inked by the fraction of that position's 255 one-byte changes that alter what the program does. Every genome in a row does exactly the same thing. If neutral history changed nothing about what is reachable next, every bar in a row would be the same bar. Rules fixed and hashed before the run.

what was asked, and what the record says

Five measurements, stated before the run, with their readings listed in advance; none has a threshold called success. Each is read by its own rule and nothing else.

X1 · neighbourhood divergence with neutral distance

The dissimilarity between an endpoint's one-mutant neighbourhood and its parent's, measured coarsely on the five classes (D5) and finely on exact signatures (Dsig), by distance target 8, 16, 32, 48, 60. Parents are the unit of inference: three walks summarised within parent by their median, uncertainty from resampling parents. No directional threshold. Pre-listed readings: (a) not distinguishable from zero at every target; (b) above zero at the first target and flat afterwards; (c) rising with neutral travel; (d) at the matched reference by target 60.

Dsig by target (mean over parents of the within-parent median): 8: 0.0013, 16: 0.0026, 32: 0.0046, 48: 0.0065, 60: 0.0077; Spearman's rho over parent medians against target 0.79 (parent bootstrap 0.76 to 0.83). D5 by target: 8: 0.0002, 16: 0.0004, 32: 0.0008, 48: 0.0012, 60: 0.0015; rho 0.64 (0.58 to 0.69). Against realised Hamming, rho 0.74; against Hamming inside the parent's expressed region, 0.52. References: unrelated parents with the identical full signature, Dsig 0.009 (0.008 to 0.010) over 60 parents; unrelated parents matched on lifespan only, 0.402 (0.304 to 0.500).

(c) rising with neutral travel, and still below the matched reference at target 60 Dsig is a lower bound on phenotypic difference: two children with the same signature may differ in ways the signature does not record.

X2 · accessibility without phenotypic change

For each endpoint, the set of signatures reachable in one mutation against its parent's: Jaccard(S) and Dsig. Decision rule fixed in advance: an endpoint's neighbourhood differs from its parent's if Dsig > 0 or Jaccard(S) < 1, provided the reproducibility check passed with every replicate distance exactly zero. The fraction differing, by target, with a parent-level bootstrap. No threshold on the size of the difference.

Reproducibility check: 141 of 141 re-evaluated neighbourhoods agreed exactly, so the rule applies as written. Endpoints whose neighbourhood differs from the parent's: at 8, 100.0% (100.0% to 100.0%); at 16, 100.0% (100.0% to 100.0%); at 32, 100.0% (100.0% to 100.0%); at 48, 100.0% (100.0% to 100.0%); at 60, 100.0% (100.0% to 100.0%). Jaccard(S) by target: 8: 0.982, 16: 0.963, 32: 0.932, 48: 0.902, 60: 0.870.

every endpoint differs from its parent, at every target The rule is sensitive by construction: one differing child among 16,319 is enough, so this fraction records that a difference exists, not how large it is; the size is X1's result.

X3 · accessible variation

Three quantities per genome, as paired differences endpoint minus parent, by target: adjacent variation (the fraction of children whose signature differs from the genome's), phenotypic richness (distinct child signatures), upward accessibility (the fraction of children that outlive the genome on probe A). Pre-listed readings: unchanged; changed in one direction across parents; changed without a consistent direction. No threshold.

Adjacent variation, in percentage points: at 8, -0.00 (-0.01 to +0.00); at 16, -0.01 (-0.02 to -0.00); at 32, +0.00 (-0.01 to +0.01); at 48, +0.00 (-0.01 to +0.02); at 60, -0.00 (-0.02 to +0.01). Richness, in distinct signatures: at 8, -0.1 (-0.4 to +0.3); at 16, -0.2 (-0.9 to +0.4); at 32, -0.0 (-1.1 to +1.0); at 48, -1.9 (-4.0 to -0.1); at 60, -3.2 (-6.0 to -0.7). Upward accessibility, in percentage points: at 8, -0.002 (-0.007 to +0.001); at 16, -0.003 (-0.012 to +0.004); at 32, -0.012 (-0.034 to +0.008); at 48, -0.003 (-0.025 to +0.019); at 60, +0.005 (-0.017 to +0.027).

adjacent variation down at target 16 (within a hundredth of a percentage point); richness down at targets 48, 60; upward accessibility unchanged at every target The word evolvability is not used.

X4 · exact against aggregate

For each endpoint–parent pair, the Jaccard overlap of the exact sets of non-neutral (position, value) substitutions, reported as the difference from its exact random-subset expectation (hypergeometric), beside D5 and Dsig, by target. The question is whether exact mutational overlap declines along the targets while the aggregate accessibility distances stay comparatively small, read beside the matched references. Reported as it appears; no binary classification.

Jaccard(M) by target: 8: 0.996, 16: 0.992, 32: 0.985, 48: 0.971, 60: 0.925; minus its expectation for independent random sets of the same sizes: 8: 0.972, 16: 0.968, 32: 0.961, 48: 0.947, 60: 0.901. Beside it, D5 8: 0.0002, 16: 0.0004, 32: 0.0008, 48: 0.0012, 60: 0.0015 and Dsig 8: 0.0013, 16: 0.0026, 32: 0.0046, 48: 0.0065, 60: 0.0077.

exact overlap 0.996 at 8 to 0.925 at 60 against about 0.024 by chance; D5 at most 0.0015, Dsig at most 0.0077 A Jaccard(M) far above its expectation means the two programs share their exact fragile positions; near it, they share only as much as strangers would.

X5 · nothing new, observation only

E2's detectors, unchanged, over every child, plus self-inspection (CODESIZE, CODECOPY, ADDRESS, EXTCODEHASH of self executed). Incidence reported. Nothing predicted, nothing rewarded. child_has_code and calls that enter code are replicated three times before being reported as more than candidates.

Children reaching each event, over 1,407 neighbourhoods: self_inspection 2,142,809, clean_halt 270,447, returns_data 219, create_executed 195, write_survives 147. Genomes with a child that left code behind: 0. Genomes with a child whose call entered code: 0.

nothing new The scorecard moves only as E2 §9 specified.

in three sentences

A program's neutral descendants, which still do exactly what it does under the frozen measurement, do not keep exactly its immediate futures: their one-mutant neighbourhoods diverge from its own, by a little at eight bytes and by more at every target after, in step with neutral distance.

The amount of adjacent variation does not change with the travel: the share of children that behave differently, and the share that outlive the genome, are the same sixty bytes away as at home, while the exact mutations that matter slowly rearrange, nine in ten still shared at sixty bytes and the rest moved.

Two programs can do the same measured thing without being in the same place: silent history does not add or remove possibility so much as rearrange which possibilities are one mutation away, changing the shape of the next step without changing the present.
